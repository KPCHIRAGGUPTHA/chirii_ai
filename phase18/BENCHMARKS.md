# Phase 18 — MiniGPT on standard public benchmarks

All runs: the college GPU PC (one RTX A2000 12 GB), 2026-11-03 to 2026-11-04. Nothing in this phase changes MiniGPT or
any release. Every number below comes from a file in `phase18/results/`; the tables are generated by `report.py`.

## In short

MiniGPT (34M parameters, 1.51B pretraining tokens) was run next to GPT-2 and four small modern models on 12 standard
benchmarks with EleutherAI's lm-evaluation-harness, the tool most published small-model numbers come from.

- **MiniGPT is at chance on the hard reasoning and knowledge tests**: ARC-Challenge, WinoGrande, OpenBookQA, MMLU
  and TriviaQA are not significantly above random guessing.
- **It is clearly above chance on the easier ones**: ARC-Easy (38.6% vs 25% random), SciQ (60.3% vs 25%), PIQA (57.0%
  vs 50%), and it predicts the last word of a LAMBADA passage 17.3% of the time.
- **Against GPT-2 (124M), the v2.0 target**: MiniGPT is significantly behind on LAMBADA (−15.2 points), PIQA (−5.5)
  and HellaSwag (−3.4); level, within noise, on 8 other tasks; and ahead on BoolQ (+11.1), where both models are
  below the "always answer yes" baseline of 62.2%, so BoolQ says little about either.
- **Instruction tuning (v1.0.1) did not improve any benchmark** and lowered LAMBADA and BoolQ. Its calculator was used
  on only 2 of 1,319 GSM8K questions; on the 500-question subset its answers are identical with or without it.
- **Modern small models are far ahead**: SmolLM2-360M and Qwen2.5-0.5B lead MiniGPT by 20–30 points on ARC-Easy,
  HellaSwag and SciQ; only Qwen2.5-0.5B (trained on up to 18T tokens) is clearly above chance on MMLU (47.7%).

## How it was run (the same for every model)

- **0-shot** on every task. Float32. Greedy decoding.
- **The 10 multiple-choice / loglikelihood tasks** use lm-eval's plain prompts for every model, base or instruct, on
  their **full** test sets.
- **GSM8K and TriviaQA** (generated answers): base models get the plain prompt; instruct models get their own chat
  format (`--apply_chat_template`; for MiniGPT v1.0.1 its own chat runtime). MiniGPT v1.0.1 ran GSM8K twice: with
  its calculator tool and with the calculator blocked. Replies are capped at 256 tokens (GSM8K) and 64 tokens
  (TriviaQA, see below).
- **Metric per task**, fixed before results were read: length-normalised accuracy for ARC, HellaSwag, PIQA, SciQ and
  OpenBookQA; accuracy for WinoGrande, BoolQ, LAMBADA and MMLU; GSM8K exact match on the last number in the reply
  ("flexible-extract"; the strict `#### N` format is recorded too, and was 0–1% for every model); TriviaQA exact match.
- **MiniGPT** runs through an lm-eval adapter written for this phase (`minigpt_lm.py`, 13 tests). Its window is 512
  tokens; longer inputs are cut from the left, never cutting the answer being scored. How often that happened:

### Generative tasks on fixed subsets

Generating answers was far slower than scoring choices: the first full TriviaQA run (17,944 questions) took 2 hours for
one small model, and the remaining work was estimated at well over 3 hours. GSM8K and TriviaQA were therefore scored on
**fixed random subsets: 500 of 1,319 GSM8K questions and 1,000 of 17,944 TriviaQA questions** (`subsets.json`,
`numpy.random.default_rng(18)`), **the same questions for every model**.

- Runs made earlier on the full sets were re-scored on exactly those items from lm-eval's own per-item verdicts
  (`subset_scores.py`); later runs used lm-eval's `--samples` option. A check on GPT-2 gave identical replies and
  identical verdicts on all 1,500 items both ways.
- The cost is precision: standard errors are about 1.6× (GSM8K) and 4.2× (TriviaQA) what the full sets would give,
  about ±0.2–1.9 points here. At these low scores that does not change any conclusion below, but small GSM8K/TriviaQA differences should not be
  read as real unless marked significant.

### TriviaQA reply cap

Answers are cut at the first newline, full stop or comma, so only the opening words are ever scored. The cap was set
to 64 tokens for every model (lm-eval's default is 256). Of the 91,365 accepted answers, 99.9% are at most 39 MiniGPT
tokens long, so 64 can only matter for an answer longer than that reproduced word for word. Checked directly:
SmolLM2-135M scored exactly 5.14% on the full TriviaQA set with either cap.

### A setup problem found and fixed

Qwen2.5-0.5B ships its own generation settings (`max_new_tokens: 2048`), and in this transformers version that
setting overrides lm-eval's length cap: its first GSM8K run generated up to 2,048 tokens per answer (median 593) while
every other model was capped at 256. Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct ships `repetition_penalty: 1.1`, which would also have
applied. Passing explicit arguments did not override them. Both Qwen models were therefore run from local copies of
the same pinned files whose `generation_config.json` keeps only the token ids, which is exactly what GPT-2 and SmolLM2
ship. A three-question check confirmed no reply over 256 tokens and plain greedy decoding. The invalid run is kept in
`results/qwen2_5_0_5b/gsm8k_invalid_maxnew2048/` and is not used.

## An honest reading

**Where MiniGPT is at chance.** ARC-Challenge, WinoGrande, OpenBookQA, MMLU and TriviaQA (both MiniGPT models), plus
GSM8K for v1.0.1. These need either facts the model never stored or several reasoning steps. GPT-2 is also at chance
on all of them except GSM8K, and so is SmolLM2-135M on MMLU: at this size, these tests mostly measure chance.

**Where MiniGPT has real signal.** ARC-Easy, SciQ, PIQA, HellaSwag (barely: +2.7 points over chance) and LAMBADA.
These reward general familiarity with ordinary English text, which 1.51B tokens of FineWeb-Edu did teach.

MiniGPT base is level with GPT-2, within noise, on 8 of 12 tasks (ARC-Easy, SciQ, and six where both are at or near
chance) and significantly behind on three: **LAMBADA (17.3 vs 32.6)**, PIQA (57.0 vs 62.5) and HellaSwag (27.7 vs 31.1). The LAMBADA gap is the
largest and the most informative: it needs the model to use a whole passage, and MiniGPT has a 512-token window,
8 layers of 512 dimensions, and about a quarter of GPT-2's parameters. SciQ (−4.1 ± 2.2) is just short of significant.
On BoolQ MiniGPT is ahead, but both models are below the 62.2% "always yes" baseline, so neither is answering
from the passage. The next foundation model is GPT-2-sized (124M parameters); a reasonable v2.0 target is to match or
beat GPT-2 on LAMBADA, HellaSwag, PIQA and SciQ, where MiniGPT is clearly behind today.

v1.0.1 is not better than its own base model on any benchmark by a significant margin except TriviaQA (+0.4: 4 of
1,000 answers right instead of 0). It is significantly worse on LAMBADA (−6.5) and BoolQ (−5.3): tuning on short

model called the calculator on only 2 of 1,319 questions, and on the 500 scored questions its replies are identical
with and without it (0.6% either way). It learned to use the tool for bare arithmetic ("What is 14+29?"), not to turn
a word problem into a calculation.

**A caution about the instruct models' TriviaQA scores.** All four instruct models score near zero (0.2–1.3%) while
their base models score up to 18.5%. With their chat format they answer in full sentences ("The flag of Libya is a
plain rectangle of…"), and exact match against the bare answer ("green") fails. That is a format effect of the
benchmark, not a loss of knowledge. On GSM8K the chat format helps: Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct reaches 24.8%.

**Reading.** Overlap is rare except BoolQ (6.6%), SciQ (3.9%) and MMLU (1.7%). Looking at the matched text
(`results/contamination_examples.txt`), most matches are **shared source text, not leaked answers**: BoolQ and SciQ
items include a Wikipedia or textbook paragraph that also appears on the web; other matches are stock phrases ("at
least twice a day, once in the morning and once at night"), answer-option layouts ("A. I only B. II only…") or
famous quotations. Some MMLU question stems do appear on the web. These rates are an upper bound for MiniGPT; they
cannot be checked for the other models, whose training data is not available. BoolQ, the task with the most
overlap, is also the one task where MiniGPT beats GPT-2; but its 59.8% is still below the 62.2% "always yes"
baseline, so it is not answering from memorised items, though a small effect cannot be ruled out. On SciQ (−4.1
vs GPT-2) and MMLU (at chance) the overlap shows no sign of helping.

## Caveats

1. **One run per model.** Phase 14 showed training-seed noise can be large for MiniGPT; these numbers are for one
   checkpoint each.
2. **Prompt format matters.** lm-eval's prompts differ from the ones in some model cards (see the CommonsenseQA case),
   so compare numbers within this report rather than with published tables.
3. **0-shot only.** Few-shot prompting usually helps larger models more than small ones.
4. **Subsets** for GSM8K and TriviaQA, as explained above, with larger standard errors.
5. **MiniGPT's 512-token window** cut 1.2% of MMLU and SciQ requests and 0.6% of BoolQ; this can only lower its scores
   slightly.
